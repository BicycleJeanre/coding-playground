# Big Room House Production Cheat Sheet

## 1. Core Genre Characteristics

Big Room House is designed for festivals and large venues:
- **Massive, festival-ready drops** — maximum impact, maximum energy
- **Simple, memorable chord progressions** — easy to feel, hard to forget
- **Build and release structure** — the whole track is about the drop
- **Heavy sidechain pump** — the supersaw chords pumping against the kick
- **Anthemic, crowd-pleasing** — accessible and universal

The goal is maximum impact at maximum scale — music that sounds massive on a festival main stage.

---

## 2. Tempo

| Style | BPM |
|---|---|
| Big Room House | 126–132 |
| Mainstage EDM | 128–132 |
| Electro House | 126–132 |

**Recommended starting point:** 128 BPM.

---

## 3. Song Structure

Big Room House is almost entirely build → drop → build → drop.

| Section | Length | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Intro | 16–32 bars | Kick + bass; DJ-friendly |
| Verse | 8–16 bars | Vocal; simple chord stabs |
| Pre-Chorus | 8 bars | Filter rising; hi-hat intro |
| Build | 16–32 bars | Rising tension; filtered leads |
| Drop | 16–32 bars | Maximum energy; supersaw leads |
| Breakdown | 8–16 bars | Stripped; vocal or atmosphere |
| Second Build | 16 bars | Rising tension again |
| Second Drop | 16–32 bars | Drop variation; often biggest |
| Outro | 16–32 bars | Strip down |

**Typical total length:** 5–7 minutes.

---

## 4. Kick Drum

### Characteristics
- Hard, punchy — festival-level kick
- Strong transient with solid low-end (60–80 Hz)
- Very tight tail
- High-passed everything else from the kick frequency

### Pattern
```
K . . . K . . . K . . . K . . .
(4-on-the-floor; rigid; no swing)
```

### Processing
- Heavy sidechain from kick to everything (especially chords)
- Transient shaper: maximum punch
- Parallel compression for density

---

## 5. Bass

### Drop Bass
- **Simple**: follows root notes of chord progression
- **Distorted mid bass** layered over a sub sine
- Heavy sidechain to kick — the pump IS the groove
- Often an aggressive synth riff (short notes, staccato)

### Verse Bass
- Lighter walking bassline
- Serves as groove support

---

## 6. Chord Progressions

Big room house keeps it simple and powerful.

| Progression | Example | Feel |
|---|---|---|
| I – V – vi – IV | C – G – Am – F | Universal anthem |
| i – VI – III – VII | Am – F – C – G | Minor anthem |
| I – IV – V – vi | C – F – G – Am | Festival classic |

- **Simple voicings** — power chords or basic triads are common
- Change every 2–4 bars during the drop
- Supersaw chords; fat unison

### Supersaw Chord Sound
1. 7+ oscillators, heavily detuned
2. High filter cutoff — bright and full
3. Hard sidechain pump from kick
4. Long attack (softens entry) → short release
5. Stereo widener for size

---

## 7. Melody / Leads

### Drop Lead
- **Short, catchy riff** — usually 2–4 bars
- Supersaw or aggressive lead synth
- Often pentatonic or simple major/minor pattern
- Hard-distorted high-frequency edge

### Build Lead
- **Filtered version** of the drop lead
- Gradually opens filter during the 16-bar build
- Often pitch-rises leading to the drop
- Reverb and delay wash before the drop hits

### Melody Design Tips
- Keep the riff **short and singable** — festival crowd must catch it instantly
- Use a **5th or octave jump** as a hook
- Add a **pitch-modulated synth** for the drop excitement

---

## 8. Builds

The build is what sells the drop. This is an art form.

### Build Elements
| Element | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Filtered lead opening | Audible tension building |
| Snare roll (16th → 32nd) | Rhythmic tension |
| Rising white noise | Energy swell |
| Riser FX | Electronic sweeping sound |
| Reverse cymbal | 1–2 bars before drop |
| Stutter / glitch | Final 2 bars |
| Bass sidechain increasing | Pump becomes more pronounced |
| Crowd noise sample | Festival vibe |

### Common Build Mistake
Do NOT over-build. The drop must feel **bigger** than the build. Use automation that peaks exactly on beat 1 of the drop.

---

## 9. Vocals

- **Hookline vocal** — short, memorable, high energy
- Often Auto-Tuned and processed heavily
- Chants: "Hey! Hey!" style crowd call-and-response
- Filtered and reverbed in the build
- Full vocal in the breakdown / verse

---

## 10. FX Elements

| FX | Usage |
|---|---|
| LFO Tool | Sidechain pump on chords |
| Riser | Pre-drop energy builder |
| Impact / Mega hit | Drop downbeat |
| Reverse cymbal | 1–2 bars before drop |
| Crowd noise | Build release FX |
| Delay | Lead melody in drop |
| Reverb | Breakdown vocals and pads |
| Distortion | Mid bass in the drop |

---

## 11. Mixdown Tips

- **Loudness**: Big room is mastered loud — -6 to -8 LUFS is common
- **Punch**: Kick must dominate; side chain must be obvious
- **High end**: Bright, sparkly — boost 8–12 kHz
- **Low end**: Keep kick and bass separated by frequency
- **Stereo width**: Chords and leads wide; kick/bass mono
- **Energy**: Parallel compression on drums for density

---

## 12. Sub-Genres at a Glance

| Sub-Genre | Key Traits |
|---|---|
| Big Room House | Festival, supersaw, massive drops |
| Electro House | Older sound; more bass-driven; distorted |
| Progressive Electro | Bridge between progressive house and big room |
| Melbourne Bounce | Bouncy kick pattern, vocal chops, playful |
| Bass House | Heavier bass; grimier; smaller rooms |
