# Techno Production Cheat Sheet

## 1. Core Genre Characteristics

Techno is defined by:
- **Industrial, mechanical aesthetic** — repetitive, machine-like rhythms
- **Dark atmosphere** — tension without resolution
- **Minimal melody** — texture and rhythm over harmony
- **Hypnotic groove** — minimal changes keep listeners in a trance state
- **Raw, gritty sounds** — distortion, noise, and processing are tools

The goal is not catharsis, but sustenance — keeping the crowd in a relentless groove.

---

## 2. Tempo

| Style | BPM |
|---|---|
| Detroit Techno | 130–138 |
| Berlin Techno | 132–140 |
| Industrial Techno | 140–150 |
| Hardcore / Gabber | 150–180 |
| Hard Techno | 140–150 |

**Recommended starting point:** 135 BPM.

---

## 3. Song Structure

Techno is DJ-oriented — long intros/outros and minimal structure shifts.

| Section | Length | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Intro | 32–64 bars | Kick alone or with sub; DJ entry |
| Groove Intro | 32–64 bars | Hats, percussion, bassline added gradually |
| Main Section A | 64 bars | Full groove; first texture layers |
| Variation B | 64 bars | One new element; one removed |
| Breakdown (optional) | 16–32 bars | Strip-down; FX and atmosphere only |
| Main Section C | 64 bars | Intensified version |
| Outro | 32–64 bars | Elements removed one by one |

**Typical total length:** 8–12 minutes.

---

## 4. Kick Drum

The kick is the most important sound in techno.

### Characteristics
- Long, deep low-end (50–70 Hz fundamental)
- Hard, sharp transient
- Minimal overtones — surgical and focused
- Often processed with saturation or distortion
- May have a slight pitch sweep from high to low on impact

### Pattern
```
K . . . K . . . K . . . K . . .
(4-on-the-floor; absolutely rigid, no swing)
```

### Processing
- Layer: click transient (sharp attack) + sub body (sine wave)
- Transient designer for extra punch
- Hard sidechain entire mix to kick
- Side-chain pumping is often extremely pronounced

---

## 5. Bass

### Characteristics
- Deep, dark, and minimal
- Often a simple motif: 1–2 notes repeated
- Sub-heavy; almost no melody
- Can be a simple sine wave, or a distorted saw

### Types
| Bass Type | Character |
|---|---|
| Sine sub | Pure, deep, club-ready |
| Distorted saw | Aggressive, industrial |
| Reese bass | Detuned, droning, classic Detroit |
| Noise bass | Filtered noise for texture |

---

## 6. Melodic Elements

Melody is secondary in techno — used for atmosphere and texture.

### Acid Lines
- Roland TB-303 style (Phoscyon, AcidLab Miami, Audiorealism ABL3)
- Single-note repetitive patterns with filter modulation
- Resonant, squelchy, distorted

### Synth Textures
- Atonal pads — dissonant, droning
- Metallic tones — bell-like but harsh
- Noise bursts — filtered rhythmically for texture
- Industrial stabs — short, punchy, arrhythmic

### Chord Use (Rare)
- When chords appear: minor, dark, unresolved
- Often just 2-note intervals (5ths, tritones)

---

## 7. Drums & Percussion

| Element | Notes |
|---|---|
| Kick | The foundation — 4/4, deep, hard |
| Clap / Snare | Beats 2 and 4; metallic, industrial |
| Hi-Hat | 16th note grid; tight and aggressive |
| Open Hi-Hat | Sparse, adds groove or tension |
| Ride | Metallic; used for movement in Berlin-style |
| Shaker / Tambourine | Subtle texture layer |
| Industrial percussion | Metal clangs, machine sounds, noise hits |

### Drum Machine References
- **Roland TR-909**: Iconic techno kick and clap
- **Roland TR-808**: Deep kick, snappy snare
- **LinnDrum**: Punchy, snappy samples
- **Custom samples**: Industrial sounds, field recordings

---

## 8. Atmosphere & Texture

Atmosphere is what separates good techno from great techno.

| Layer | Description |
|---|---|
| Dark pad | Low-frequency drone; barely audible but felt |
| Noise texture | Filtered white/pink noise riding the track |
| Metallic texture | High-frequency industrial shimmer |
| FX ambience | Hall reverb tails from hits |
| Rhythmic noise | Noise burst filtered in rhythm |

---

## 9. Automation

Techno evolves through automation, not arrangement changes.

- **Filter cutoff** — gradual opening/closing over 16–32 bars
- **Reverb size** — builds and collapses
- **Distortion amount** — adds intensity without new sounds
- **Volume swells** — slow pad automation
- **Hi-hat decay** — changes character without adding elements

---

## 10. FX Elements

| FX | Usage |
|---|---|
| Distortion / Overdrive | Kick processing, bass, synths |
| Short reverb | Snare, clap — metallic space |
| Long dark reverb | Pads, atmospherics |
| Delay | Sparse hits; creates space |
| Bitcrusher | Digital degradation; industrial feel |
| Comb filtering | Metallic resonance on percussion |
| Tape saturation | Glues mix; adds warmth and grit |

---

## 11. Mixdown Tips

- **Mono low end**: All sub content mono
- **Kick volume**: Dominant but not clipping — leave headroom
- **Harsh frequencies**: EQ 2–4 kHz carefully to avoid fatigue
- **Sidechain**: Aggressive sidechain from kick is expected
- **Low mids**: Cut 200–400 Hz on most elements to keep mix clean
- **Loudness target**: -6 to -8 LUFS for club masters (loud!)

## 12. Sub-Genres at a Glance

| Sub-Genre | Key Traits |
|---|---|
| Detroit Techno | Soulful undertones, classic drum machines, groove |
| Berlin Techno | Minimal, industrial, very long sets |
| Industrial Techno | Noise, aggression, dissonance |
| Hard Techno | Distorted kicks, aggressive energy |
| Minimal Techno | Extreme restraint; micro-variations |
| Dub Techno | Reggae-inspired delays, warm and spacious |
| Acid Techno | Heavy TB-303 acid lines |
