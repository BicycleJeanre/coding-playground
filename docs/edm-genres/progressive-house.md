# Progressive House Production Cheat Sheet

## 1. Core Genre Characteristics

Progressive House bridges the gap between house and trance:
- **Long, patient builds** — tension unfolds over many bars
- **Emotional, atmospheric feel** — melodic but understated
- **Flowing basslines** — melodic and groove-driven simultaneously
- **Layered synth textures** — pad-heavy, ambient backgrounds
- **Subtle drops** — not as aggressive as big room; the "drop" is more of a release

The goal is a journey — music that evolves over the course of the track.

---

## 2. Tempo

| Style | BPM |
|---|---|
| Classic Progressive House | 126–130 |
| Modern Progressive House | 126–132 |
| Progressive Trance border | 130–136 |

**Recommended starting point:** 128 BPM.

---

## 3. Song Structure

Progressive house rewards patience. Builds are long.

| Section | Length | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Intro | 32 bars | Minimal — atmosphere and kick |
| Verse / Groove | 32 bars | Bassline and groove established |
| Build 1 | 16–32 bars | Melody introduced, filter opens |
| Breakdown | 16–32 bars | Emotional peak, stripped drums |
| Drop | 32 bars | Full energy, climax |
| Second Build | 16 bars | Re-tension |
| Second Drop | 32 bars | Variation on first drop |
| Outro | 32 bars | Gradual exit |

**Typical total length:** 7–9 minutes.

---

## 4. Kick Drum

### Characteristics
- Balanced kick — between house punch and trance power
- Moderate low-end, defined transient
- Slightly longer tail than classic house
- Often tuned to the root note

### Pattern
```
K . . . K . . . K . . . K . . .
```

---

## 5. Bass

### Characteristics
- Melodic, flowing bassline — not just root notes
- Often uses the chord tone + movement between chords
- Sub-heavy with a warm mid character
- Sidechain to kick (moderate pump, not aggressive)

### Synth Choices
- Serum / Sylenth1 saw wave with warm low-pass filter
- Slightly detuned for width
- Automate filter for gradual opening during builds

---

## 6. Chord Progressions

Progressive house tends toward **minor** for emotion, with some major for uplift.

| Progression | Example | Feel |
|---|---|---|
| i – VI – III – VII | Am – F – C – G | Classic progressive |
| i – iv – VII – III | Am – Dm – G – C | Melancholic, flowing |
| I – V – vi – IV | C – G – Am – F | Pop-influenced, uplifting |
| i – VII – VI – i | Am – G – F – Am | Dark, circular |

- Chords change every 4–8 bars
- Lush voicings with 7ths and extensions

---

## 7. Melody / Leads

### Lead Synth
- Emotional, understated — less in-your-face than trance
- Often a detuned supersaw with soft filter
- Comes in fully in the breakdown and drop
- Long, sustained notes with expressive automation

### Pads
- Central to progressive house
- Massive reverb, long attack and release
- Layer multiple pads: a dark undertone + a bright top layer
- Automate the filter cutoff for the build

### Arpeggios
- Sparse, under-the-surface — not as dominant as in trance
- Often filtered and buried under the pads

---

## 8. Drums & Percussion

| Element | Notes |
|---|---|
| Kick | 4/4; tuned, moderate punch |
| Clap / Snare | 2 and 4; slightly roomy |
| Hi-Hat | 16th groove; tight |
| Percussion loop | Organic-feeling shakers, congas |
| Cymbal swells | Used in build-ups |
| Reverse reverb | Builds tension before drop |

---

## 9. Builds & Transitions

The build-up is the art form of progressive house.

### Build Techniques
- **Filter sweep**: Gradual high-pass open on entire mix or synths
- **Pitch rise**: Riser element sweeping upward
- **Drum fill**: Snare rolls and hi-hat build
- **Tension melody**: Sustained note that doesn't resolve until the drop
- **White noise sweep**: Classic progressive element
- **Stutter / gate**: Final 2 bars before drop; syncopated cuts

### Breakdown Techniques
- Strip all drums
- Retain or introduce the emotional pad and melody
- Build reverb size on the melody
- Use a long, building pad swell

---

## 10. FX Elements

| FX | Usage |
|---|---|
| Risers | Long 8–32 bar sweeps into drops |
| Cymbal swell | Last 4 bars before drop |
| Downlifter | Enter breakdown |
| Reverse pad | Adds eerie tension |
| White noise | Classic sweeping build FX |
| Delay throws | Vocal/melody tail into breakdown |

---

## 11. Mixdown Tips

- **Space**: Progressive house needs air — don't over-compress
- **Low end**: Clear separation between kick and bass
- **Mids**: Guard against muddiness; broad cut at 200–400 Hz on competing synths
- **Reverb**: Generous on leads and pads; don't be shy
- **Loudness target**: -10 to -12 LUFS; more dynamic than big-room
