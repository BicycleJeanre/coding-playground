# UK Garage Production Cheat Sheet

## 1. Core Genre Characteristics

UK Garage (UKG) is characterized by:
- **Swung, skippy rhythms** — the defining "2-step" beat
- **Chopped and pitched vocals** — the vocal is central and creatively processed
- **Sub-heavy bass** — deep 808-style bass with movement
- **Soul and R&B influence** — warm, soulful, emotional underpinning
- **British urban identity** — rooted in London club culture

The goal is that irresistible skip in the rhythm that makes you move differently — not four-on-the-floor, but a shuffle.

## 2. Tempo

| Style | BPM |
|---|---|
| Classic UK Garage | 130–135 |
| Speed Garage | 130–135 |
| 2-Step Garage | 130–136 |
| Grime (descendant) | 140 |
| UK Funky | 130–135 |

**Recommended starting point:** 132 BPM.

## 3. Song Structure

| Section | Length | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Intro | 16–32 bars | Groove established; minimal |
| Verse | 16 bars | Main vocal; full groove |
| Pre-Chorus | 8 bars | Energy rise; vocal chops |
| Chorus / Hook | 16 bars | Full energy; vocal hook |
| Bridge | 8–16 bars | Stripped; atmospheric |
| Verse 2 | 16 bars | Variation |
| Final Chorus | 16–32 bars | Biggest version |
| Outro | 16 bars | Strip down |

**Typical total length:** 4–6 minutes.

## 4. Drums — The 2-Step Beat

The **2-step pattern** is the defining feature of UK Garage.

### Classic 2-Step Pattern (1 bar, 1/16th notes)
```
Kick:  K . . . . . . . K . K . . . . .
Snare: . . . . S . . . . . . . S . . .
Hat:   h h h h h h h h h h h h h h h h
```

### Characteristics
- Kick does NOT fall on every beat — it's syncopated and skipping
- Snare falls on beats 2 and 4 but often with syncopation
- Hi-hats are fast, even 16ths — sometimes swung
- The kick pattern is where variation lives

### Swing / Shuffle
- Apply **slight swing** (52–58%) to hats and percussion
- Do not over-swing — it should feel skippy but not loose

### Drum Sounds
- **Kick**: Tight, punchy TR-909 or similar; not too deep
- **Snare**: Clappy, bright; layered with a TR-909 clap
- **Hi-hats**: Fast, bright; 16th note grid
- **Percussion**: Optional shaker, rim shot accents

---

## 5. Bass

### Characteristics
- Deep sub bass (808-style or sine)
- Melodic — bass riff with movement
- Often punctuated (short notes with silence) — adds to the skippy feel
- Heavy sidechain to kick

### Speed Garage Bass
- Sub bass with a "bump" — 808 bass tuned down low
- Punchy, short notes with lots of space

### 2-Step Bass
- More melodic, following vocal and chord
- Can include a running bassline with 16th-note syncopation

---

## 6. Chord Progressions

UK Garage draws heavily from **soul, R&B, and gospel**.

| Progression | Example | Feel |
|---|---|---|
| I – vi – IV – V | C – Am – F – G | Classic soul |
| i7 – IV9 – VII7 – III7 | Am7 – Dmaj9 – G7 – Cmaj7 | Jazz-influenced |
| I – V – vi – III – IV | C – G – Am – Em – F | Emotional, pop |
| i – VI – III – VII | Am – F – C – G | Warm, minor |

- Use **7th and 9th chord extensions** for soulfulness
- Chords often played on Rhodes or warm pad

---

## 7. Vocals

Vocals are the **centrepiece** of UK Garage.

### Characteristics
- Soulful, R&B-influenced singing
- **Chopped and pitched** vocal chops used as rhythmic/melodic elements
- Pitch-shifted up (chipmunk style in early UKG)
- Full verses with a catchy hook

### Vocal Chop Technique
1. Record or source a vocal sample
2. Chop into individual syllables or phonemes
3. Pitch-shift up 3–7 semitones (classic UK garage sound)
4. Re-arrange chops into a rhythmic riff
5. Layer with reverb and light chorus

### Lyrical Themes
- Love, relationships, night life, aspiration
- London club culture
- Call-and-response MC patterns (in sets)

|---|---|

| Warm pad | Background harmonic support |
| Pluck synth | Lead melody hook |
| String element | Emotional background swell |
| High-pitched vocal chop | Rhythmic hook |

---

## 9. FX Elements

| FX | Usage |
|---|---|
| Pitch-shift (vocal chop) | Up 3–7 semitones for classic UKG |
| Reverb (room) | On snare and vocals |
| Delay (1/8 dotted) | On vocals and synths |
| Filter (gentle) | Subtle opening on builds |
| Chorus | Vocal warmth; pad width |
| Tape saturation | Warmth on Rhodes and bass |

---

## 10. Mixdown Tips

- **Groove**: The skippy kick rhythm must be the focal point
- **Vocals**: Front and center; the lead element
- **Bass**: Deep but controlled; sidechain to kick
- **Warmth**: Tape saturation or gentle tube saturation on drum bus
- **Stereo**: Modest — UKG doesn't require extreme width
- **Loudness target**: -12 to -14 LUFS; moderate dynamics
