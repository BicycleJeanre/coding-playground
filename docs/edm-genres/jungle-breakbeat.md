# Jungle / Breakbeat Production Cheat Sheet

## 1. Core Genre Characteristics

Jungle and Breakbeat are the original rave genres based on chopped breaks:
- **Chopped breakbeats** — sampled drum loops sliced, rearranged, and layered
- **Reggae / ragga influence** — vocal samples, bassline dub, dancehall elements (Jungle)
- **Frenetic energy** — fast, complex, dense rhythms
- **Sub bass and ragga bass** — deep bass lines with reggae-influenced movement
- **Rave culture roots** — late 1980s/early 1990s UK rave scene

Jungle and Breakbeat are closely related but distinct:
- **Jungle**: Reggae/ragga influenced; darker; 160–168 BPM
- **Breakbeat**: Broader category; more funk-influenced; 120–145 BPM

---

## 2. Tempo

| Style | BPM |
|---|---|
| Jungle | 160–168 |
| Drum & Bass (descendant) | 170–180 |
| Big Beat | 120–140 |
| Nu Skool Breaks | 125–140 |
| Breakbeat House | 125–135 |
| Acid Breaks | 130–145 |

**Recommended starting point:** 165 BPM (Jungle), 132 BPM (Breakbeat).

---

## 3. Song Structure

| Section | Length | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Intro | 16–32 bars | Atmospheric; break enters gently |
| Verse / Drop 1 | 32 bars | Full break; bass; vocal sample |
| Break | 16 bars | Stripped atmosphere; tension |
| Drop 2 | 32 bars | Variation on break; new element |
| Mid-break | 16 bars | Regroup; atmosphere |
| Drop 3 | 32 bars | Intensified variation |
| Outro | 16–32 bars | Strip to break, then fade |

**Typical total length:** 5–8 minutes.

## 4. Drums — The Break

The break is the defining feature.

### The Amen Break
The most sampled drum loop in electronic music history.
- From "Amen, Brother" by The Winstons (1969)
- A 4-bar drum solo at ~136 BPM (sped up for jungle/DnB)

### The Think Break
- From "Funky Drummer" era; classic funk break
- Punchy, raw, lo-fi

### Break Manipulation Techniques
1. **Pitch-shift up** to speed the break to target BPM (without time-stretch artifacts)
2. **Chop and rearrange** slices in a sampler (MPC, Battery, Simpler)
3. **Layer** multiple breaks for density
4. **Pitch individual hits**: raise/lower specific snares and kicks
5. **Add effects**: reverb on snare, flanger on kick, distortion on full break
6. **Ghost notes**: add extra light hits between main hits

### Drum Aesthetic
- **Raw, gritty, lo-fi** — high compression, some distortion
- Real-sounding — not quantized perfectly
- Humanized: micro-timing variations preserved

### Creating the Groove
| Hit | Character |
|---|---|
| Kick | Deep, dark, punchy |
| Snare | Crackling, organic, raw |
| Hi-hats | Riding, sometimes cymbal wash |
| Ghost notes | Extra light hits for texture |

## 5. Bass

### Jungle Bass
- Deep **dub/reggae-influenced bassline**
- Rolling, melodic, rhythmically active
- Sub frequencies dominant (50–80 Hz)
- Often uses reggae bass patterns (off-beat emphasis)

### Bassline Pattern (Reggae-influenced)
```
. . . . R . . . . . . . R . . .
(Root note on offbeats — classic reggae feel)
```

### Ragga Bass
- Deeper, more compressed
- 808-like in sub character
- Sometimes sampled from reggae records

### Breakbeat Bass
- More funk-influenced
- Syncopated, bouncy
- Following the break groove

---

## 6. Melody & Samples

Jungle and Breakbeat are fundamentally **sample-based** genres.

### Vocal Samples
- Ragga MCs (jungle): "Wickedness!" "Junglist!" style
- Soulful R&B hooks
- Spoken word, news clips (breakbeat)
- Heavily chopped, pitched, and reverbed

### Melodic Elements
- Reggae-influenced organ or piano chords
- Dub bassline variations
- Rave-era synth stabs
- Sampled brass or strings

### Sampling Ethics
- Always clear samples if releasing commercially
- Consider using sample packs or create original interpretations

## 7. Atmosphere

| Element | Character |
|---|---|
| Dub delay | Long reggae-style tape echo |
| Reverb | Cavernous, Jamaican sound system |
| Vinyl noise | Gritty, warm, lo-fi |
| Atmospheric samples | Nature, city, speech |
| Sub bass drones | Felt in the chest, not heard |
| Rasta / reggae FX | Dub mixing effects: echo drops, reverb swells |

## 8. FX Elements

| FX | Usage |
|---|---|
| Dub echo | Repeating delay on bassline; reggae-influenced |
| Spring reverb | Snare and break character |
| Distortion / saturation | Break crunch; bass harmonics |
| Pitch-shift | Speed breaks up to target BPM |
| Time-stretch | Stretch pads/atmospheres |
| Flanger | Classic rave-era effect on breaks |
| Phaser | Sweeping texture on synths |
| Sidechain | Subtle bass pumping to kick |

## 9. Big Beat (Breakbeat Subgenre)

Big Beat is a commercial-friendly form of breakbeat.

### Characteristics
- 120–140 BPM
- Rock-influenced elements (distorted guitars, rock drums)
- Simple, accessible structure
- Heavy, crunchy breaks
- Catchy hooks
- Famous artists: Chemical Brothers, Fatboy Slim, Crystal Method

---

## 10. Mixdown Tips

- **Breaks**: The crunch and grain IS the sound — don't over-clean
- **Sub bass**: Mono; low-passed at 80–100 Hz; separate from break low end
- **Vinyl feel**: Gentle tape saturation or vinyl simulation adds authenticity
- **Compression**: Heavy parallel compression on breaks for punch
- **Loudness target**: -8 to -10 LUFS for club; jungle can be fairly loud

## 11. Sub-Genres at a Glance

| Sub-Genre | Key Traits |
|---|---|
| Jungle | Reggae/ragga influence; raw breaks; 160–168 BPM |
| Drum & Bass | More refined jungle; less reggae; 170–180 BPM |
| Big Beat | Commercial; rock + breaks; 120–140 BPM |
| Nu Skool Breaks | Modern production values; 125–140 BPM |
| Acid Breaks | TB-303 acid + breakbeats |
| Breakbeat House | House BPM with broken beats |
| Breaks | General term for any breakbeat-based music |
