# Future Bass Production Cheat Sheet

## 1. Core Genre Characteristics

Future Bass is defined by:
- **Emotional, bittersweet chord writing** — melancholic but uplifting simultaneously
- **Super-saw chords with sidechain** — the "chords pumping" sound is the signature
- **Pitched-up vocal chops** — processed, chopped, and pitched vocals everywhere
- **Sub bass drops** — contrasting silence before a heavy sub entrance
- **Bright, shimmery high end** — sparkle and air in the upper register

The goal is emotional impact — music that makes you feel nostalgic and euphoric at once.

---

## 2. Tempo

| Style | BPM |
|---|---|
| Classic Future Bass | 130–150 |
| Melodic Dubstep crossover | 140–145 |
| Chill Future Bass | 110–130 |
| Trap crossover | 130–145 |

**Recommended starting point:** 140 BPM.

---

## 3. Song Structure

Future bass songs are often pop-structured.

| Section | Length | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Intro | 8–16 bars | Calm; piano or minimal elements |
| Verse | 16 bars | Subdued; vocal or guitar |
| Pre-Chorus | 8 bars | Build; hi-hat pattern; energy rises |
| Chorus / Drop | 16–32 bars | Full supersaw + sidechain |
| Post-Chorus | 8 bars | Short continuation |
| Verse 2 | 16 bars | Slight variation |
| Bridge | 8–16 bars | Atmospheric; emotional peak |
| Final Chorus | 16–32 bars | Biggest version of the drop |
| Outro | 8 bars | Strip down |

**Typical total length:** 3–5 minutes.

---

## 4. Drums

### Kick
- **808 kick** or deep sub kick
- Punchy, with a long sub tail (tuned)
- Half-time pattern (similar to trap influence)
- Sometimes absent for bars; used for contrast

### Snare / Clap
- Bright, snappy
- On beat 3 (half-time feel) or beats 2 and 4
- Often layered with a clap for brightness
- **Reverb**: long, roomy

### Hi-Hats
- **Rolls and rapid 1/32nd note patterns** — a defining future bass element
- Accent on offbeats
- Multiple hi-hat types layered
- Ghost hat shots for groove

### Hi-Hat Roll Pattern Example
```
h . h h . h h h . h . h h h . h (1/16th and 1/32nd subdivisions)
```

---

## 5. Bass

### Sub Drop
The entrance of the sub bass after a break is a key future bass moment.

- Pure sine sub or tuned 808
- Tuned to the root note of the drop chord
- Heavy sidechain to the kick — aggressive pump
- Layer with a mid-range element for body (100–300 Hz)

### Mid Bass
- Can be a pluck or growl shape
- Distorted slightly for harmonics
- Follows the root chord movement

---

## 6. Chords — The Most Important Element

Future bass is fundamentally about chord writing.

### The Super-Saw Chord
1. Synth: 7+ unison voices, heavily detuned
2. Chords played as whole notes or half notes
3. **Hard sidechain** to kick — the "pumping" is essential
4. Bright filter — high cutoff, lots of high harmonics
5. Layer with a warm pad underneath

### Voicing
- Use **inversions** and **extended chords**: maj7, add9, sus2, sus4
- Spread voicings across 2–3 octaves
- Higher inversions sound more open and emotional

### Common Progressions

| Progression | Example | Feel |
|---|---|---|
| I – V – vi – IV | C – G – Am – F | Universally emotional |
| I – vi – IV – V | C – Am – F – G | Classic, nostalgic |
| vi – IV – I – V | Am – F – C – G | Minor-first, emotional |
| I – IV – vi – III | C – F – Am – Em | Bittersweet |

- **Modulate** for effect: drop up a half-step for the final chorus
- Mix major and minor chords for bittersweet quality

---

## 7. Melody & Leads

### Melodic Characteristics
- Singable, emotional — often follows a pop sensibility
- Built from chord tones + chromatic movement
- Often uses a vocal, pluck, or bright lead

### Lead Sounds
| Sound | Technique |
|---|---|
| Vocal chop | Pitch vocal samples up 2–5 semitones; chop into notes |
| Pluck synth | Sharp attack, medium decay; brighter than trap |
| Piano | Emotional, acoustic-feel counterpart to synth chords |
| Bell synth | Bright, shimmery, emotive |
| Flute / Whistle | Airy, top-end sparkle |

---

## 8. Vocal Chops

The vocal chop is a signature future bass sound.

### Techniques
1. **Sample a vocal** (sung syllable, vowel, or word)
2. **Chop** it — use only 10–50 ms slices
3. **Pitch** the chops to follow the chord notes
4. **Layer** multiple chops at different pitches simultaneously
5. **Reverb**: very large, bright hall
6. **Pitch up**: the "chipmunk" pitched vocal effect is common

### Creating a Chord from Vocal Chops
- Take a single vowel sound
- Pitch it to each note of the chord (e.g. C, E, G for C major)
- Play all pitches simultaneously
- Result: a "vocal chord" — dreamy and unique

---

## 9. Atmosphere & Pads

- **Lush pad** under the chords: slow attack, huge reverb
- **Airy high-end shimmer**: bell-like overtones
- **Modulated textures**: slowly evolving pads
- **Silence before the drop**: a moment of silence/near-silence before the drop is a key trick

---

## 10. FX & Sound Design

| FX | Usage |
|---|---|
| Sidechain (LFO Tool) | Hard pump on chords synced to kick |
| Reverb (bright, large) | On vocals, chords, leads |
| Chorus | Widening chords and leads |
| Pitch-up | Vocal chops pitched up 2–8 semitones |
| Glitch/stutter | Pre-drop tension, fills |
| High-pass sweep | Opening filter into the chorus |
| Riser | Pre-drop energy build |
| Silence/empty bar | Before the drop; maximum tension |

---

## 11. Mixdown Tips

- **High-end air**: EQ boost at 10–16 kHz on master bus; future bass should be bright
- **Sidechain pump**: Audible and pronounced — this IS a feature
- **Sub bass**: Mono and clean; tuned 808 with clean transient
- **Vocal chops**: Dedicate space; don't bury them
- **Loudness target**: -8 to -10 LUFS; streamable and punchy
